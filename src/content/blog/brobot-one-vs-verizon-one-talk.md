# Brobot One vs Verizon One Talk: Which Small Business Phone System Wins?

If you're shopping for a business phone system, you've probably seen Verizon One Talk. It's a well-known option from a major carrier, but is it the right fit for a small business that needs more than just dial tone? How does it compare to a system built specifically for capturing every lead and never missing a call?

We've done the research. Here's an honest, detailed comparison to help you decide.

## Verizon One Talk: Deep Dive

Verizon One Talk is a cloud-based business phone system that lets you use one number across multiple devices, desk phones, mobile phones, and tablets. It integrates with Verizon's wireless and wireline services, so if you're already a Verizon customer for cell or internet, bundling can simplify billing and support.

**What Verizon One Talk offers:**

- **Multi-device ring:** Ring your desk phone, cell, and tablet from one number
- **Extension support:** Add extensions for team members
- **HD voice and video:** Quality calling and video conferencing
- **Mobile app:** Answer and make calls on the go
- **Integration options:** Works with some CRMs and productivity tools (often via add-ons or third-party connectors)

**What Verizon One Talk lacks for lead-focused businesses:**

- **No built-in AI answering.** When you can't pick up, callers go to voicemail. Research shows 85% of callers won't call back after voicemail, so you're losing leads the moment the line goes silent.
- **No built-in CRM.** You need a separate tool (Salesforce, HubSpot, etc.) to track leads and follow-ups. That means manual log-in, switching between systems, and potential data gaps.
- **No 24/7 automated lead qualification.** There's no system to answer after hours, capture contact info, or schedule callbacks when you're closed.
- **Call transcription:** Available as an add-on or through limited integrations, often with additional per-seat or per-minute costs.

**Pricing context:** Verizon One Talk pricing varies by plan, number of lines, and add-ons. You'll typically pay per user per month, plus any extras for transcription, advanced features, or CRM integration. It's worth getting a formal quote for your team size.

**Best for:** Businesses already embedded in Verizon's ecosystem who primarily need reliable multi-device calling with minimal extras. If your main concern is "can my team answer from anywhere?" and you're okay with voicemail as the fallback, One Talk can work.

## Use Case: Who Should Choose Which?

**Choose Verizon One Talk** if:

- You're deeply invested in Verizon (wireless, internet, etc.) and want to consolidate
- You mainly need multi-device calling and don't mind voicemail as the fallback
- You already have a CRM you love and just need dial tone
- Your call volume is low and missed calls aren't a critical issue

**Choose Brobot One** if:

- You need to capture every call and never send anyone to voicemail
- You want AI qualifying leads and logging to CRM automatically
- You're in an industry where speed to lead and 24/7 availability matter (contractors, HVAC, dental, etc.)
- You'd rather have phone + CRM in one platform than manage multiple tools

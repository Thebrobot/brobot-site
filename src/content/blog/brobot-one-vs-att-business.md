# Brobot One vs AT&T Business: AI Phone, CRM, and 24/7 Answering Compared

AT&T Business is a well-known name in business communications. They offer voice, internet, and unified communications for organizations of all sizes. But if you're a small business that needs more than basic calling,AI answering, CRM, and real lead capture, how does AT&T stack up?

We've done the research. Here's an honest, detailed comparison to help you choose.

## AT&T Business: Deep Dive

AT&T Business offers a range of voice, internet, and unified communications services. Their business phone solutions include traditional landlines, VoIP, and collaboration tools like Microsoft Teams integration. If you're already an AT&T customer for internet or other services, bundling can simplify billing and support.

**What AT&T Business offers:**

- **Reliable voice and data services**, carrier-grade infrastructure
- **Multi-line and extension support** for teams
- **Mobile integration**, answer and make calls from your cell
- **Enterprise-grade reliability**, designed for larger deployments with high call volume
- **Integration options** with Microsoft 365, CRM platforms, and productivity tools (often via add-ons or professional services)

**What AT&T Business lacks for lead-focused small businesses:**

- **No built-in AI answering.** When you can't pick up, callers go to voicemail. Research shows 85% of callers won't call back after voicemail, so you're losing leads the moment the line goes silent.
- **No built-in CRM.** You need a separate tool (Salesforce, HubSpot, etc.) to track leads and follow-ups. That means manual data entry, switching between systems, and potential gaps when someone forgets to log a call.
- **No 24/7 automated lead qualification.** There's no system to answer after hours, capture contact info, or schedule callbacks when you're closed.
- **Call transcription:** Available as an add-on or through limited integrations, often with additional per-seat or per-minute costs. Not standard.

**Pricing context:** AT&T Business pricing varies by plan, number of lines, and add-ons. You'll typically pay per user per month, plus any extras for transcription, advanced features, or CRM integration. Enterprise plans can be complex; small businesses may find the sales process and pricing structure geared toward larger accounts.

**Best for:** Businesses already embedded in AT&T's ecosystem who primarily need reliable dial tone, multi-line support, and enterprise-grade uptime. If lead capture and 24/7 answering aren't top priorities, AT&T can deliver solid baseline communications.
